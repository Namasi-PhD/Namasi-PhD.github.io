HADOF

Industrial Scale Applications on Today’s QPUs

The Hamiltonian Auto-Decomposition Optimisation Framework splits optimisation problems far larger than any quantum processor into circuits that fit on it, then recombines the answers. It has already assembled a bacterial genome on IBM hardware.

How it works

Solve a few qubits at a time, share what each piece learns

Sweep 1
Toy run computed live in this page: 24 variables, 3-qubit circuits, a Boltzmann sampler standing in for QAOA. Shade shows each variable’s probability of being 1. Not benchmark data.
  1. DecomposeSplit the QUBO into small groups of variables that each fit in one circuit.
  2. Solve on the QPURun each group as a small circuit. Every other variable enters through its current probability, so each piece sees the rest of the problem.
  3. Share and repeatUpdate the probabilities from the measured samples and sweep again. The circuit size never grows with the problem.
  4. RecombineAssemble thousands of full candidate solutions and keep the best. Any sampler plugs in: QAOA, quantum annealing, SA, FALQON.

Flagship application

Quantum genome assembly of the P. aeruginosa bacterial chromosome

Exploiting today’s quantum computers for real optimisation workloads: real nanopore reads, a standard assembly pipeline, and the hard ordering step solved by HADOF on ibm_torino.
